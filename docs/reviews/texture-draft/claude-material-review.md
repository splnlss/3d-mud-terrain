**Verdict.** The v2 render is closer in hue direction but further in overall read: it now looks like dark, uniformly gritty chocolate soil under flat overhead light, where the photo is a pale warm clay with smooth satin faces, clustered dark crumbs, a wet sheened channel floor, and grazing backlight. Most of the gap is shader and lighting, not geometry, and nothing here is a measured match.

**Four highest-impact changes**

1. **Channel floor material (shader).** In the photo the channel is not water. It is smooth wet mud, slightly lighter than the banks, with sheet-flow streaks and a strong glancing specular. Both drafts render it as a flat near-white plane, and the v2 script explicitly skips objects tagged as water. Give that object the same clay albedo lightened a step, roughness around 0.25 to 0.35, a faint streak normal along flow, and let it catch the key light. This single change fixes the most alien element in the frame.

2. **Reflectivity of the matrix (shader).** The authored roughness sits near 0.9 almost everywhere and only drops in the broad "wet" mask, so the banks read as dry dust. The photo clay has a satin sheen on every smooth face at grazing angles. Lower the dry baseline to roughly 0.55 to 0.65, keep wet zones lower, and raise the specular level a little. Bump strength of 0.8 over a 0.1 m range plus a second global noise bump also scatters highlights into grain, which is why no sheen survives. Remove the global noise bump from flat faces.

3. **Texture scale and distribution (shader, with some placement).** The 8 cm noise pores and the small lobes read as sandpaper at this camera distance. In the photo, smooth faces dominate, and crumbs come in clusters roughly a twentieth to a thirtieth of hill height, concentrated mid-slope and on shaded sides, with fine vertical rills on steep faces. Drive crumb and pore detail by a slope mask and a broad low-frequency cluster mask, and add a slope-aligned anisotropic rill normal for steep faces. Consider a larger projection repeat so lobes are hill-relative rather than fixed at 32 m.

4. **Albedo value and lighting.** The v2 albedo is too dark and too saturated, and the baked residual shading is what produces the uniform speckle. Lighten and desaturate toward pale ochre tan, and let value variation come from cavity, wetness, and the lit crumbs rather than from the colour image. Separately, the photo's look depends on a low grazing backlight with rim highlights and a soft sky fill. Keep the fixed camera and light for the comparison render, but add a second "look" render with that key, because no material will read as the photo under flat overhead light.

**Geometry, not shader.** Sharp conical peaks and knife ridges versus the photo's rounded, slumped domes. Dark flat plate clasts scattered uniformly versus rounded crumb clusters. A hard channel edge against a plane versus gradual beveled banks. These should be fixed in the source master and clast mesh, not in materials.

**Worth retaining.** World-space box projection with no UV seams. Relief authored independently of colour, which is the right principle even though the colour still carries shading. Roughness driven by a wetness map. The recorded settings file and fixed camera for before-and-after comparison. The move toward warm hue, just pulled back to a lighter value.
